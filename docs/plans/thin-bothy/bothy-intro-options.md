# Home: "What a bothy is" - three other ways to say it

Status: options only, nothing built. The built version is the reading wheel (the words in big type, rolled past a
centre line as you scroll). The owner's complaint was that the old hut drawing and console mock beside the words did
not connect to the product. Each idea below replaces the words-only wheel with something that tells you something
true, and each keeps every sentence we already have (the lead, the four lines of the Bothy Code, the close).

## 1. The door that opens onto the console

A single drawn door, centre screen, shut. As you scroll, the Bothy Code lines are written on it one at a time, big,
as if carved. After the fourth ("Turn no one away.") the door swings open and what is behind it is the real
first screen of the console (the Overview), at rest, marked EXAMPLE. The point it makes: "turn no one away" is also
how the product behaves - the page is open on your own machine, nothing to sign up for.

Motion: door leaf rotates about its edge over about 600 ms once the last line is read (scroll starts it, it
finishes on its own clock); the lines before it only fade in and out. Reduced motion: door already open.

## 2. One hut, four rules, four things the product does

A split of the screen that stays pinned. Left: the four lines of the Bothy Code, one lit at a time. Right: under each
line, the one fact about the software that keeps the same rule, with its number from `facts.ts`: "Leave it as you
found it" beside "the reader never writes"; "Keep numbers down" beside "38 MiB across three containers";
"Respect other users" beside "one open port"; "Turn no one away" beside "free, MIT licence". Nothing invented: only
rows that already exist in the proven list.

Motion: as the lit rule changes, the right-hand fact slides up from below and the previous one slides out. Counting
numbers tick up once. Reduced motion: all four pairs shown as a plain two-column list.

## 3. The shelter on a map of your own machine

A flat black "map": a small rectangle labelled with the machine's name (EXAMPLE), the three containers as three dots
inside it. The text lines scroll past as captions; each time a rule is read, the matching part of the map lights up
(walls = "four walls and a roof" lights the rectangle; "nobody at the door" lights the one open port; "volunteers
keep them up" lights the update line). It reads like a legend being unrolled, so the words explain the drawing
instead of decorating it.

Motion: scroll-scrubbed, one lit element per line, a thin line drawing itself between the caption and its part.
Reduced motion: the finished map with its legend underneath.
